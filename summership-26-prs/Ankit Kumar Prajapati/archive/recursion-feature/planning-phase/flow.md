# Flow

This document describes the sequence and flow of the learning module.

## 1. Case Study

- Present the case study as an interactive narrative with reflection questions at appropriate points.
- Encourage the learner to think before revealing the next step of the solution.
- At the end of the case study, include a **Summary** section.
- The Summary will consist of a sequence of reflection questions. Each question is shown first. After the learner clicks **Next**, its explanation is revealed, followed by the next reflection question.
- The objective of this stage is to help the learner understand the underlying problem-solving approach **without introducing the term "recursion."**

---

## 2. Reveal the Concept

- The final reflection from the Summary acts as a bridge between the case study and the formal concept.
- Reveal that the approach used to solve the case study is called **recursion**.
- Introduce the following concepts:
  - Recursion
  - Base case
  - Recursive case
  - Waiting for the answer (return phase)
- Map each concept back to the corresponding events in the case study so that learners can connect the formal terminology with what they have already experienced.
- Reinforce the concepts through 4–5 conceptual MCQs.

---

## 3. Recursion in Code

- Show how the same idea can be implemented as a recursive function.
- Explain how the base case and recursive case appear in code.
- Provide one code-completion exercise where learners drag and drop the missing statements into the correct positions.
- End the feature by suggesting the learner to watch a short (6–7 minute) visual explanation video.

---

> **Note**
>
> Topics such as **head recursion**, **tail recursion**, **call stack**, and other advanced recursion concepts are intentionally excluded from this version to keep the feature focused and beginner-friendly. These topics can be documented as future enhancements for anyone extending this module.
