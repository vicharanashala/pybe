# Newton's Prism

When Isaac Newton passed a beam of white light through a glass prism, he revealed that white was not a pure color, but a composite of the entire spectrum. The prism acted as an analytical tool, splitting the unified beam into its fundamental components.

In programming, integers often hide composite information. A single number can represent a combination of true/false flags, a compressed pixel color, or hardware states. Bitwise operators—AND, OR, XOR, and shifts—are your prism. They allow you to mask out the noise and extract the exact bits you need.
