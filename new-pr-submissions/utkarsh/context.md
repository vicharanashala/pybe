# Context: The Savage Mountain (Python Sets)

## Educational Context

This module adheres to PyBe's story-first pedagogy: friction arises naturally in the narrative, the learner resolves the issue using common-sense reasoning, and Python syntax is introduced solely as the name for the pattern they discovered.

Each scene's dilemma is structured so that the *only* clean resolution is the computational concept being taught:
- **Scene 1 (Darjeeling):** Three climbers each packed rope. A pack mule cannot carry three redundant coils. Deduplication is required before moving forward.
- **Scene 2 (The Fork):** Two convoys arrive with independent lists. The local merchant requires a single unified order without duplicate entries—which is precisely a union.
- **Scene 3 (Skardu Checkpost):** An inspector will only clear goods appearing on both the cargo manifest and the government permit—an exact intersection.
- **Scene 4 (Baltoro Glacier):** Days into the trek, food has been consumed. Determining the remaining sled load requires subtracting consumed items—a set difference.
- **Scene 5 (The Bottleneck):** In the death zone, survival depends on an instant check: is oxygen in the kit or not? This isolates boolean membership.
- **Scene 6 (K2 Summit):** Looking at notes with counts ("rope: 2, stove: 1") reveals the boundary of sets, posing the question that naturally motivates dictionaries.

## Story Context

The narrative follows nineteen-year-old Tara, a mountaineering trainee from Darjeeling, and Pasang, a seasoned Sherpa quartermaster. The story begins in Darjeeling at the Himalayan Mountaineering Institute (HMI), home of Himalayan expedition training, before traveling to the Karakoram highway, Skardu, Baltoro Glacier, and the high slopes of K2.

## Non-Claims

- **Fictional Characters:** Tara and Pasang are fictional figures created for pedagogical narrative purposes.
- **Geographic Routes:** Darjeeling is the historical training home of the expedition, not part of the Karakoram approach route. The two roads in Scene 2 refer to the historical southern route via Skardu and northern routes across Central Asian passes.
- **Approximate Altitudes:** All elevations are marked as approximate ("approx.") for educational context.
